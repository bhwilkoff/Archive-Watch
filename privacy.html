Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 22, 2026

Archive Watch is a free Apple TV app for browsing and watching public-domain films and television from the Internet Archive. It is built to respect your privacy completely: there is no advertising, no tracking, and nothing about you is ever sold or shared. The apps collect nothing at all. The website keeps one aggregate counter, described below, that stores nothing about you.

What we collect

The apps collect nothing at all. Archive Watch’s Apple TV, iPhone, iPad, Mac, Android, Fire TV and Roku apps have no analytics of any kind. We never receive, see, or store any information about you or how you use them.

The website keeps one counter, described immediately below. It is the only thing we run, and it is written so that it cannot hold anything about a person even if we wanted it to.

The website counter

archivewatch.org counts how often each part of the site is opened, and which public-domain films are opened and played. It stores rows of exactly this shape and nothing else:

  • 2026‑09‑14  |  /browse  |  412 — a date, a part of the site, a count.
  • 2026‑09‑14  |  the‑grapes‑of‑wrath‑1940  |  played  |  7 — a date, a film, whether it was opened or played, a count.

What it never stores, deliberately and permanently: your IP address, any cookie, any session or visitor identifier, your browser or device, the page you came from, your country, or the time of day. Nothing in it can be joined to a person, to another row, or to a second visit — including by us. We cannot tell whether two rows are the same person, and we cannot tell whether you have ever visited.

Playlists you share are never counted. A shared playlist travels inside its own web address, so that address is discarded in your browser before the counter is contacted at all.

The counter runs on Cloudflare Workers. It is the only server-side thing this project operates; there is no other backend, no advertising, no third‑party analytics service, and nothing is ever sold or shared. The counts exist so we know which films people are finding useful.

Information stored on your device

To make the app useful, some information is stored locally on your Apple TV and never leaves it except as described under iCloud sync below:

  • Your Continue Watching progress (where you stopped in a title).
  • Your Favorites and any playlists you create.
  • Your preferences, such as the mature-content filter and autoplay settings.

Optional Sign in with Apple & iCloud sync

Signing in is entirely optional. Browsing and playback work fully without an account. If you choose to Sign in with Apple, it is used only to sync your own Favorites, playlists, and watch progress across the Apple TVs in your household, using your personal iCloud private database. That data lives in your own iCloud account — we have no access to it. The name and email associated with your Apple ID are handled by Apple and are never sent to us.

You can remove this synced data at any time from Settings → Account → Delete Account in the app, which deletes your records from your iCloud private database and signs you out.

Watch Together Studio: connecting a YouTube or Twitch account

Archive Watch can broadcast a public-domain film, with your camera and microphone, to your own YouTube or Twitch channel. This is entirely optional and off by default: nothing in the rest of the app requires it, and if you never open Watch Together Studio, no account is ever connected and none of this section applies to you.

Watch Together Studio: what the YouTube permission is used for

To start a broadcast, Archive Watch asks for one Google permission, https://www.googleapis.com/auth/youtube. It is used for exactly these operations on your own channel, and nothing else:

  • Read your channel to show you which channel you are about to broadcast to, and to check that live streaming is enabled on it before you press Go Live.
  • Create a live stream and a live broadcast with the title and privacy setting you chose in the app.
  • Bind them together and start the broadcast, which is what puts you on air.
  • End the broadcast when you stop the show.
  • Read the live chat of that broadcast, so your audience’s messages can be shown on screen while you present. Archive Watch never posts a chat message.

There is no narrower Google permission that allows an application to create and start a live broadcast. youtube.readonly cannot write, and youtube.upload covers uploaded videos rather than live events, so it cannot start one either. Archive Watch never reads your existing videos, never uploads a video, never edits or deletes anything on your channel, never touches your subscriptions, playlists or comments, and never acts on any channel other than the one you choose at sign-in.

Watch Together Studio: how that access is protected

  • The sign-in never passes through us. You sign in to Google directly, in a system browser window the app cannot read (ASWebAuthenticationSession), using OAuth 2.0 authorization code flow with PKCE. Archive Watch never sees your Google password, and there is no Archive Watch account, password or profile of any kind.
  • The token stays on your device. The access and refresh tokens are stored in the Apple Keychain with the protection class kSecAttrAccessibleAfterFirstUnlockThisDeviceOnly — so they are encrypted at rest, unavailable until the device has been unlocked after a restart, never synchronised to iCloud and never copied to another device.
  • We operate no server that could receive it. Archive Watch has no backend. The only server-side thing this project runs is the anonymous counter described above, which has no authentication and no user data. Your token is sent to googleapis.com and to nowhere else, over HTTPS.
  • Your stream key is never stored and never displayed. It is fetched from YouTube at the moment you go live, held in memory for that session only, and discarded when the show ends. It is never written to disk, never shown on screen, and never written to a log — a test in our build checks every error path for it before each release.
  • Nothing is logged about you. Archive Watch has no analytics, no crash reporting service and no telemetry, on any platform. Your channel name, your token and your viewers’ chat messages are never recorded anywhere outside your device.
  • The video itself goes straight to the platform. Your camera, your microphone and the film are encoded on your own device and published directly to YouTube over RTMPS (TLS). No Archive Watch server, and no third party, sits in the path.

Watch Together Studio: keeping it only as long as you want it

Sign out in Watch Together Studio deletes the token from your Keychain immediately; nothing of it remains. You can also revoke Archive Watch’s access at any time from your own Google account at myaccount.google.com/permissions, which takes effect whether or not the app is installed. Because we hold no copy on any server, there is nothing else to delete and no request to make of us. Deleting the app removes the token with it.

Watch Together Studio: Limited Use of Google user data

Archive Watch’s use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Specifically: data obtained through these permissions is used only to provide the broadcasting feature you asked for; it is never transferred to anyone else except as necessary to provide that feature, to comply with applicable law, or as part of a merger or acquisition with your consent; it is never used for advertising of any kind; it is never sold; it is never used to develop, improve or train any generalized or non-personalized artificial intelligence or machine learning model; and no human ever reads it, because it never leaves your device.

Watch Together Studio: Twitch

Connecting Twitch works the same way and is protected the same way: an OAuth device-code sign-in you complete on twitch.tv, a token held only in your device’s Keychain under the same protection class, used only to fetch your own channel’s stream key and ingest server so the app can publish to it. You can revoke it at twitch.tv/settings/connections.

Network requests to public services

To show artwork, descriptions, cast, and to stream video, the app makes requests directly from your device to these public services:

  • The Internet Archive (video and metadata)
  • The Movie Database (TMDb) (posters, cast, synopses)
  • Wikidata and Wikimedia Commons (fallback metadata and images)
  • The Library of Congress (fallback imagery)

These requests carry only what any internet request carries (such as your IP address) and are handled by those services under their own privacy policies. Archive Watch sends them no information that identifies you.

Children

Archive Watch does not collect personal information from anyone, including children. A mature-content filter is on by default.
