# Orphaned Films — what it does, and what Archive Watch could take from it

*Researched 2026-09-26 from the live site (orphanedfilms.com), its shipped
bundles, and its public MIT repository `amponce/archive-movie-browser`
(README, CHANGELOG through 2.3.0 of 2026-09-25, `api/`, `mcp/`, `src/`).*

## What it is

An independent, web-only React/Vite app on Vercel, begun 2025-12-08 (four months
before Archive Watch), 139 stars, active daily, open to outside contributors. It
streams archive.org files directly and enriches them from TMDb, with an offline
small model ("Jev") deciding which film each upload is. It fetches nothing from
Archive Watch. Its centre of gravity is **television**: 27 always-on channels on
one global clock, published as IPTV feeds.

## Ranked recommendations

Each item: what they do → what we have today → what we would build. Ranked by
fit with "Why we build" (does it invite curiosity, connection, a deliberate
choice?) against cost under our $0-to-run and privacy constraints.

### 1. Lists with a reason for every film
**Them:** a list is one JSON file — title, blurb, curator, and films in order,
**each with a one-line note** ("the stunts nobody would insure"). Anyone can add
one by pull request, and every list is also a channel.
**Us:** Collections are franchises/series (Decision 139); editorial picks live in
`featured.json` and are starved — `editors-picks` never reaches Home (owner item
4, 5 picks against a floor of 9).
**Build:** curated lists with a per-film note, rendered as a Home row and a list
page on every platform, sourced from `shared/editorial/lists/*.json` and editable
in `/curate/`. The note is the point: it tells a viewer *why* to watch, which is
the "invite to engage" test in its purest form, and it gives Editor's Picks a
supply. Community lists by pull request are a natural second step.

### 2. Channels on one clock, shareable, and built from lists
**Them:** each channel plays its lineup from a fixed global moment, so "what is
on" is identical for everyone and a link opens the same minute for anyone.
Sixteen more "stations" are generated weekly by rule (a genre × a span of
decades). Your own "channel 0" is built from films you add, shareable by link.
**Us:** Channels + an EPG exist on every platform, but `ChannelScheduler` anchors
each broadcast day at **6:00 AM local**, so two viewers in different time zones
see different programs and a shared moment is impossible.
**Build:** (a) anchor schedules to UTC so a channel is one timeline worldwide;
(b) "Tune in with me" — a link/QR to a channel at now, which with (a) needs no
server at all and is Watch Together's lightest form; (c) every curated list (#1)
and any playlist can play as a channel; (d) rule-generated weekly stations from
the rights-KEEP catalog.

### 3. IPTV feeds: M3U playlists + an XMLTV guide
**Them:** `/api/tv/playlist.m3u`, `/api/tv/channels.m3u`, `/api/tv/guide.xml`, so
VLC, Kodi, Jellyfin, Plex, TiviMate and Channels DVR can play the channels.
**Us:** none. We already publish a Roku Search feed limited to rights-KEEP films
(Decision 113).
**Build:** static M3U + XMLTV files generated by the pipeline and published on
GitHub Pages (no server, no cost), **rights-KEEP films only**, mirroring
Decision 113. Reaches every home-media setup we will never build an app for.
Pairs with #2(a): a feed only works if the clock is global.

### 4. An MCP server: let an assistant find films
**Them:** a hosted Model Context Protocol endpoint (`search_films`,
`browse_films`, `get_film`, `list_collections`, `whats_on`, a `movie_night`
prompt) so Claude/Cursor can search and hand back links that play.
**Us:** none.
**Build:** an MCP endpoint on the existing Cloudflare Worker (free tier; no new
infrastructure) over the published index: search, film details with rights
basis, More Like This with its reason, what is on each channel. Answers carry
archivewatch.org links. Frame it as a research companion — "films about the
Dust Bowl before 1930" — not a recommender.

### 5. Any archive.org address opens here
**Them:** swap `archive.org` for their domain and it works (`/details/<id>`,
collections, a user's lists `/details/@someone`); a pasted archive.org link in
search opens what it points at; an archive.org RSS or query link runs that
query.
**Us:** item pages at `/item/<id>/`; the curator tool accepts a pasted link; the
viewer's search does not.
**Build:** `/details/<id>` on archivewatch.org forwarding to the survivor's item
page (via `aliases.json`); a pasted archive.org link in every platform's search
opens that film if our catalog keeps it, and says plainly when it does not
("not in Archive Watch — its rights could not be confirmed").

### 6. "Under 90 minutes" and a runtime filter
**Them:** a length filter and an "Under 90 minutes" row; "noir you can finish
tonight".
**Us:** no runtime filter or row anywhere (checked).
**Build:** a runtime facet in Browse and an "Under 90 minutes" Home row on every
platform, computed in the pipeline (Decision 050). Helps someone choose on
purpose for the time they actually have.

### 7. Scrub previews from archive.org's own thumbnails
**Them:** hovering the scrub strip shows the frame and time, using the
per-minute thumbnails archive.org already keeps for each item.
**Us:** Roku has trick-play BIFs; the other players are not verified to show
frames while scrubbing through our resource-loader assets.
**Build:** measure first per platform; where frames are missing, feed
archive.org's per-item thumbnails to the web player's scrubber and to
Android/Apple where the platform allows.

### 8. A report path for a wrong film or poster
**Them:** a "Wrong poster?" link opening a pre-filled GitHub issue with the
identifier, the upload title and the guess.
**Us:** catalog quality is guarded by pipelines and the curator tool, but a
viewer who spots a wrong match has nowhere to say so.
**Build:** "Something wrong with this film?" on Detail → a pre-filled GitHub
issue (web) or an email/URL hand-off (apps). No server, no account, no
analytics, and every report becomes a test case for the matcher.

### 9. A clear removal-request page
**Them:** `/takedown` explains that files are the Internet Archive's, how to have
an upload removed from the site, with a form; takedowns cover every surface.
**Us:** the rights audit hides what we cannot justify, but there is no public
path for a rights holder.
**Build:** a short `/takedown` page and a hide marker registered with the
reconcile (Decision 083) so a removal holds across every surface and feed. Good
practice for a public catalog and for store review.

### 10. Critics beside the audience
**Them:** the TV screen shows TMDb's audience score and Rotten Tomatoes or
Metacritic from OMDb.
**Us:** IMDb/TMDb ratings; OMDb is already a metadata fallback and its response
carries a `Ratings` array.
**Build:** store the critics' score in the pipeline and show it only where a
rating is already shown, subject to the essential-information rule.

### 11. Smaller ideas worth a look
- **A daily "Tonight" pick with a sentence of why**, from a hand-picked list:
  our social program already writes such lines; the apps could show the day's.
- **Home that follows the day and the hour** (e.g. short films in the morning,
  a feature at night) — only if it stays a suggestion, not a feed.
- **Honest subtitle status on every player**: "Subtitles: English (on) · Arabic"
  or "No subtitle file with this upload".
- **Uploads that hold many films** (one item, a folder of features): they show
  each file as its own film. Worth measuring how many such items our catalog
  treats as one.
- **"Sound over a black picture" fallback**: they skip to the next file when an
  mp4 plays audio with undecodable (DivX-era) video. Check that Decision 077's
  fallback and the codec audit cover this case on every platform.
- **An offline judge for shelf "strays"**: their small model marks films that do
  not belong in a collection (a modern studio film in Film Noir) at sub-cent
  cost. Our rights audit covers the legal half; a "belongs here" check would
  cover the editorial half for curated shelves.

## Already in Archive Watch (no need to copy)
Folding duplicate uploads (Decision 040, now with every copy selectable), More
Like This (Decision 139, ranked with a reason), continue watching, Channels + EPG
(but see #2), watching together (rooms, now pinned to the host's copy — Decision
143), subtitles from the upload (Decision 039), search by real title (FTS), a
private analytics console (Pulse), adult-content filter by default (Decision
105), native apps on every TV and phone platform.

## What not to copy
- **Their rights posture.** Lists and channels include *RoboCop*, *The Silence
  of the Lambs* (1991) and *The Spanish Prisoner* (1997); the site hides only
  films from the last 25 years. Our rights audit (Decisions 027, 114, 137, 140)
  is a difference to keep.
- **Their analytics.** They count minutes watched per film and per channel,
  searches, filters and clicks, with a per-tab session id. Our published promise
  is that the apps send nothing (privacy.html, Decision 142); nothing above
  requires changing that.
- **Browsing all of archive.org.** They open any collection live, unaudited. Any
  archive.org address we accept must resolve to a catalog film we keep, or say
  why not (#5).
