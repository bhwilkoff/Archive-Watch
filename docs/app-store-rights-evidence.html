Archive Watch — Content Rights & Sourcing Documentation

Supporting documentation for App Review Guideline 5.2.3 (Legal)

Submission ID: 5b4a6d1e-0ca0-44e3-9d41-b3722de28bd8  ·  App: Archive Watch (iOS / iPadOS)  ·  Developer: Ben Wilkoff  ·  ben@learningischange.com  ·  archivewatch.org

This document explains where Archive Watch’s content comes from and why it does not infringe any third party’s rights. In short: Archive Watch is a dedicated viewer for the public-domain moving-image collections of the Internet Archive (archive.org), a registered 501(c)(3) nonprofit digital public library. All playable media streams directly from the Internet Archive’s own public servers; the app hosts no media and accesses no proprietary or commercial streaming service.

1. What the app is

Archive Watch is a cinematheque-style browser and player for public-domain moving images — feature films, classic television, newsreels, silent cinema, and animation — that the Internet Archive already makes freely available to the public. The app is, in effect, a specialized client for the same openly-accessible works any web browser can watch at archive.org. It is free, with no ads and no in-app purchases.

2. Where the content comes from

  • Hosted and served by the Internet Archive. Every playable title streams directly from the Internet Archive’s public servers (the archive.org/download/… endpoints it provides for public access). Archive Watch hosts, caches server-side, and re-streams nothing.
  • No proprietary or commercial service is involved. There is no integration with, or scraping of, any paid or subscription catalog (no Netflix/YouTube/etc.). The app accesses only the Internet Archive’s openly-published public collections.
  • Collections drawn from include the Internet Archive’s curated public-domain film libraries, e.g. archive.org/details/feature_films. Internet Archive Terms of Use: archive.org/about/terms.php.

3. Why the content does not infringe third-party rights

Every playable item is either:

  • in the public domain — by expiration of the copyright term, by failure to renew, or by publication without the copyright notice that U.S. law then required; or
  • openly licensed — released by its creator under a Creative Commons or CC0 license that expressly permits redistribution.

Public-domain works have no rights-holder whose rights could be violated, and Creative Commons / CC0 works are licensed precisely for this kind of access.

4. We proactively screen out anything that might still be under copyright

Before publishing the catalog the app uses, we run an automated copyright rights audit that excludes any item not confidently free. The decision is anchored on each work’s own license metadata at the Internet Archive and on U.S. public-domain rules:

First publishedTreatment
Before 1929Public domain by age — kept.
1929–1963Within the Internet Archive’s curated public-domain film collections — kept.
1964–1977The documented “public-domain by notice / renewal defect” era (e.g. Night of the Living Dead) — kept where appropriate.
1978 and laterExcluded by default. Kept only if the work’s own Internet Archive record carries a genuine CC0 / Creative Commons license. A bare “Public Domain Mark,” a missing license, or an uploader claim is not treated as a rescue — those items are hidden.

Modern branded commercials / advertisements (1995 and later) and screen-recordings / rips / compilations are excluded regardless of any uploader tag. The audit produces a per-item evidence manifest — the Internet Archive URL, publication year, license URL, and the basis for inclusion — for every title in the app. We can provide this manifest in full on request.

6. Metadata / “discovery” services

Posters, synopses, and cast information are drawn for visual enrichment only — never for the video stream — from The Movie Database (TMDB), Wikidata, Wikimedia Commons, and the Library of Congress, used within each provider’s published API terms. As a non-commercial app we display the attribution TMDB requires (“This product uses the TMDB API but is not endorsed or certified by TMDB,” with the TMDB logo) on the in-app About / Attribution screen. These services supply metadata only.

7. Available on request

  • The complete per-title rights manifest (Internet Archive URL, year, license URL, inclusion basis) for the catalog.
  • Any additional documentation or in-app change that would help — for example, we can surface the public-domain provenance and source link even more prominently on each title’s detail screen.
