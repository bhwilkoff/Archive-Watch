# Amazon Appstore — Content Policy appeal (Archive Watch 1.42.6)

**File at:** https://developer.amazon.com/support/cases/new
**Type of inquiry:** Appstore
**Category:** App Submission and Certification
**Topic:** App Content Policy Review Results

Rejection received 2026-09-11: Primary Validation PASS, Functionality
Validation PASS, **Content Policy Validation FAILED** — "Your inapp icon
appears to re-distribute an existing app from a third party as your own."

---

## Subject

Archive Watch (com.archivewatch.app) v1.42.6 — icon is our own work from a
1902 public-domain film; requesting review of the IP determination

## Body

Hello,

Archive Watch version 1.42.6 (package `com.archivewatch.app`, publisher
Learning is Change, Inc.) was not published, with the finding that our in-app
icon "appears to re-distribute an existing app from a third party as your
own." We would like to give you the provenance of that artwork, because we
believe this is a case of a very widely used public-domain image being
mistaken for a third-party app.

**We are the original and only publisher of Archive Watch.** It is not a
re-distribution of anyone else's app. The same app, under the same name,
publisher and icon, is published by us on:

- Apple App Store — https://apps.apple.com/us/app/archive-watch/id6776697407
- Google Play — https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.archivewatch.app
- Roku Channel Store — https://channelstore.roku.com/details/12a571a872732e40fe8d1d6c59f3849f:5ec8f46dcec2324229c8d096ea23c089/archive-watch
- Our own site — https://archivewatch.org

The source code is public at https://github.com/bhwilkoff/Archive-Watch,
including the icon asset and its history.

**The icon is our own composition built from a public-domain film frame.** It
is a still of the "man in the moon" from *Le Voyage dans la Lune* (*A Trip to
the Moon*), directed by Georges Méliès, first released in **1902**. We placed
that still inside a film-strip frame with sprocket perforations that we
created; the composition, framing and surround are our own work.

That film is in the public domain in the United States and worldwide:

- It was **published in 1902**, long before any US copyright term that could
  still be running. Every work published before 1930 is in the public domain
  in the United States.
- Georges Méliès died in **1938**. Under life-of-the-author-plus-70, the film
  entered the public domain in its country of origin in **2009**.
- The still is hosted as a public-domain work on Wikimedia Commons and is
  distributed as public domain by the Internet Archive, which is the source
  our app is built around.

We chose that particular frame deliberately: Archive Watch is a free,
ad-free, non-commercial viewer for the Internet Archive's public-domain film
collection, and the first film most people meet there is Méliès. The icon is
a statement about what the app contains.

**Why we think the automated match occurred.** That Méliès frame is one of
the most reproduced images in cinema history and appears in the branding of a
number of unrelated film apps, websites and products. A visual-similarity
check against an app that also uses the same 1902 public-domain still would
produce exactly this finding. Similarity to another app that used the same
public-domain source is not redistribution of that app, and no third party
holds exclusive rights to a 1902 Méliès frame.

We hold all rights necessary to distribute this app and its artwork: the
underlying film frame is public domain, and every other element of the icon
was made by us.

If it would help, we are glad to provide the layered icon source, the
original still with its Wikimedia Commons provenance, or anything else you
need. If Amazon's position is nonetheless that we should not use this frame,
please tell us and we will ship a different icon rather than hold up the
release — but we would prefer not to, because the same icon is already live
and approved on four other platforms, and changing it only on Fire TV would
make our app look like the impostor.

One note on urgency, with apologies for raising it. Version 1.42.6 exists to
fix a compatibility fault that hides Archive Watch from most Fire TV devices:
the previous build declared `minSdkVersion 29`, which excluded the entire
Fire OS 7 generation. Several Fire TV owners have publicly reported being
unable to install it, and this submission raises device support from 38 to
91. Those users are waiting on this build.

Thank you for taking another look.

Ben Wilkoff
Learning is Change, Inc.
